# Power Systems Analysis → An Original System for Our Game

*Studying the great "analyzable" power systems in fiction and games, extracting why they work, and drafting an original element + monster system for our XR card battler. Goal: a system people argue about, theorycraft, cosplay, and build lore around.*

---

## Part 1 — The meta-principle (the single most important finding)

Across every beloved, discussed-to-death power system, the same law appears:

> **Limitations are more interesting than powers.** What a force *can't* do, and what it *costs*, is what makes it analyzable and dramatic.

This is Brandon Sanderson's Second Law, and it's echoed by MTG's design team ("built-in weakness into each color is core to the game") and by Nen (abilities get stronger the more you *restrict* them). If our elements can each do a bit of everything, nobody will ever discuss them. If each element is *great* at one thing and *helpless* at another, players will spend years arguing matchups and building identities around it.

Three flavors of constraint to design with (Sanderson's framing):
- **Limitations** — things the power *cannot do* (Fire can't heal).
- **Weaknesses** — things enemies can *exploit* (Fire "burns out" late-game).
- **Costs** — what you *sacrifice* to use it (Shadow damages its own wielder).

Everything below is built on this.

## Part 2 — The systems, analyzed

### Magic: The Gathering — the Color Pie ⭐ (most important reference for us)
[Value of Pie (Mark Rosewater)](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/value-pie-2003-08-18-0) · [Color wheel explained (Draftsim)](https://draftsim.com/mtg-color-wheel/)
- Five colors (White, Blue, Black, Red, Green) are **philosophies**, not just visuals: White = order/community, Blue = knowledge/control, Black = power-at-any-cost, Red = freedom/emotion/impulse, Green = nature/instinct.
- **Each color is deliberately bad at some things** — Red has burst but "runs out of gas"; Blue controls but is slow. The weakness *is* the identity.
- **Allied vs enemy colors:** adjacent colors on the wheel are ideological allies, opposite ones are enemies. This single geometry generates factions, rivalries, and deckbuilding tension.
- **The central tension:** the color pie pushes you to want *many* colors; the mana system punishes playing *too many*. That pull is the whole game.
- **Steal:** elements as philosophies with hard can't-do boundaries + an allied/enemy wheel.

### Hunter × Hunter — Nen ⭐ (best "personality + restriction" system)
[Nen chart explained (Beebom)](https://beebom.com/hunter-x-hunter-nen-chart-explained/) · [Magic Systems Wiki](https://magicsystems.fandom.com/wiki/Nen_(Hunter_%C3%97_Hunter))
- Six aura types on a hexagon (Enhancement, Emission, Transmutation, Conjuration, Manipulation, Specialization); **your type is determined by your personality** (tested via "water divination").
- **Neighbors on the hexagon are easy for you; the opposite type is nearly impossible** — a built-in aptitude/limitation grid.
- **Vows & Limitations = power:** voluntarily binding yourself with a strict rule (and a punishment for breaking it) *massively amplifies* the ability. Risk buys strength.
- **Steal:** tie a player's element to *personality/playstyle*; let players take self-imposed **Oaths** (restrictions) for amplified power — huge for both depth and drama ("he swore a Blood Oath!").

### Pokémon — the Type Chart (cleanest "this beats that")
- 18 types in a web of super-effective / not-very-effective / immune relationships. Instantly teachable ("water beats fire"), infinitely deep at the competitive layer.
- **Steal:** a clear, memorizable matchup layer so a newcomer understands advantage at a glance, but with enough types that mastery is deep.

### Avatar: The Last Airbender — Bending (elements as *culture & identity*)
- Four elements, each a **whole civilization, philosophy, and fighting style** (Water = change/community, Earth = endurance/stubbornness, Fire = drive/power, Air = freedom/detachment). Sub-skills branch off (bloodbending, metalbending, lightning).
- **Steal:** make each element a *people and worldview*, not just a damage type — this is where cosplay, factions, and lore come from.

### Genshin Impact — Elemental Reactions (elements that *combine*)
[Elemental reactions guide (RPG Site)](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/10318-genshin-impact-elements-elemental-combos-reactions-burst-and-their-effects)
- Seven elements, but the magic is that **applying two in sequence triggers named reactions** (Pyro+Hydro = Vaporize, Pyro+Cryo = Melt, Pyro+Electro = Overload). **Order matters** (Pyro-then-Hydro ≠ Hydro-then-Pyro).
- **Steal:** a **reaction system** where combining your element with your opponent's (or your ally's) creates emergent effects — this is spatial-combat gold and gives spectacle + theorycraft.

### Naruto — Chakra Natures (element *fusion* as bloodline)
- Five natures in a rock-paper-scissors (Fire<Water<Earth<Lightning<Wind<Fire). Rare users **combine two into a new nature** (Water+Wind sub-styles; bloodlines make Ice = Water+Wind, Lava = Fire+Earth).
- **Steal:** advanced players unlock **fusion elements** (a prestige/rare identity) — aspirational, lore-rich, and a great rarity hook for cards.

### One Piece — Devil Fruits (unique *collectible* powers)
- Powers as **unique collectible items** (Paramecia/Zoan/Logia), each one-of-a-kind, each with the famous universal cost: **the user can never swim.** A single shared limitation ties a wildly diverse system together.
- **Steal:** the collectible framing (each legendary creature/power feels singular) + one elegant universal cost that binds the whole cosmology.

### Yu-Gi-Oh — Attributes + Types (the card-game baseline)
- Monsters have an **Attribute** (LIGHT, DARK, EARTH, WATER, FIRE, WIND, DIVINE) *and* a **Type/clan** (Dragon, Warrior, Spellcaster, Fiend…). Attributes are broad; types drive **tribal synergy**.
- **Steal:** the **two-axis** design — an *element* axis (governs reactions/matchups) and a *clan* axis (governs synergy/tribal decks). This doubles design space and identity.

### Mushoku Tensei — Rank Ladder + Chantless Casting
- Seven-rank ladder (Elementary→God) for every school; casting needs **incantations** that scale with power; the elite skill is **chantless casting** (skip the chant = instant).
- **Steal:** a clean **rank ladder** for power tiers, and **incantations as an optional, skill-expressive layer** (recite for power/drama; master players cast silently). Fits our summon-trigger design perfectly.

## Part 3 — The principles we extract

1. **Every element must be helpless at something** (Sanderson/MTG). Identity = weakness.
2. **Tie elements to personality & culture** (Nen/Avatar) so players *self-identify* ("I'm a Tide summoner").
3. **An allied/enemy wheel** (MTG) auto-generates factions, rivalries, and lore.
4. **A clear matchup layer** (Pokémon) for teachability + a deep layer for mastery.
5. **Reactions from combining elements** (Genshin) for emergent, spatial spectacle.
6. **Fusion elements as prestige** (Naruto) for aspiration and rarity.
7. **Two axes: element + clan** (Yu-Gi-Oh) to multiply identity and synergy.
8. **Voluntary restrictions grant power** (Nen) for skill expression and drama.
9. **One universal cost** (One Piece) to bind the cosmology and give it a "rule."
10. **A rank ladder + optional incantations** (Mushoku Tensei) for progression and theater.

## Part 4 — DRAFT v0.1: The "Aspect" System (react to this, don't marry it)

> **DECIDED (locked):**
> 1. **Grounded elemental Aspects** — each is a real element on the surface (Fire, Water, Earth, Wind, Life, Shadow) with a philosophy underneath (Avatar-style). Newcomers grasp them instantly; lore and creatures hang off them naturally.
> 2. **Oaths & Reactions are an ADVANCED layer, not core.** A full duel plays cleanly using just summons, attacks, defenses, and the Aspect matchup wheel. Reactions and Oaths are powerful *optional* tools that reward mastery — so onboarding stays gentle while veterans get a deep meta. The core loop must be fun and complete without ever touching them.

**Cosmology (the story hook):** Long ago there was one primal force — **the Origin**. It shattered into six **Aspects**. Every summoner is *attuned* to one Aspect; that attunement shapes their personality, their monsters, and what they fundamentally *cannot* do. The eternal friction between Aspects is the world's history, its wars, and its factions. (This gives writers infinite material: why do Ember and Tide clans feud? why is Umbra outlawed in some nations? etc.)

**The universal cost (the One-Piece-style binding rule):** every summoner channels through their monsters — *a summoner with no monster on the field cannot cast their higher Aspect powers.* Your creatures are your conduit. Lose them all and you're nearly powerless. (This also enforces our design goal: the player supports monsters, monsters do the fighting.)

### The six Aspects (element + philosophy + hard limit + wheel)

| Aspect | Element | Philosophy / personality | Great at | **Cannot do (limit)** | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ember** | Fire | Passion, momentum, "now over later" | Fastest, highest early burst | Cannot heal or shield | Burns out — no late-game sustain |
| **Tide** | Water | Patience, change, adaptation | Countering, redirecting, transforming | Cannot deal big raw damage | Weak when forced to act first |
| **Bastion** | Earth/Stone | Permanence, protection, resolve | Walls, endurance, zone control | Cannot reach far or burst | Slow; poor mobility |
| **Gale** | Wind/Air | Freedom, cunning, detachment | Speed, evasion, hit-anywhere reach | Cannot hold ground / defend | Fragile — lowest health |
| **Verdant** | Life/Nature | Cycles, community, growth | Healing, swarms, resource generation | Cannot produce elite single units early | Loses to burst before it "grows" |
| **Umbra** | Shadow/Decay | Ambition, cost, "ends justify means" | Debuffs, drains, **revive from graveyard** | Cannot directly heal | **Costs your own life force** to power |

**The wheel (allies & enemies):** arrange them in a hexagon — Ember→Gale→Umbra→Tide→Verdant→Bastion→(Ember). Adjacent = allies (natural two-Aspect splashes), opposite = enemies (Ember vs Tide, Gale vs Bastion, Umbra vs Verdant). Opposites can *technically* be combined but trigger volatile reactions.

**Reactions (Genshin-style, spatial spectacle):** when two Aspects meet on the battlefield (your spell + opponent's monster, or your two monsters):
- Ember + Gale → **Firestorm** (spreads across the field)
- Ember + Tide → **Scald** (big one-time burst, but consumes both — enemies pay to make it)
- Tide + Gale → **Tempest** (control/displacement)
- Bastion + Verdant → **Overgrowth** (fortified, healing terrain)
- Umbra + anything → **Corruption** (converts/curses the reaction)
- *Order matters* (like Vaporize): who applies first changes the result → deep theorycraft.

### Two axes (Yu-Gi-Oh style)
- **Aspect** (the six above) → governs reactions, the matchup wheel, and player identity.
- **Clan** (cross-cutting creature families — e.g., Drakes, Constructs, Wraiths, Beasts, Titans) → governs *tribal synergy*, independent of Aspect. So an "Ember Drake" and a "Tide Drake" both benefit from Drake synergies but play totally differently. Doubles the design space.

### Player identity & progression
- Each player picks a **primary Aspect** (their identity and starter power); at higher ranks they may **splash an allied secondary** (two-Aspect summoners), and rare masters unlock a **Fusion Aspect** (Naruto-style prestige: e.g., Ember+Bastion = **Magma**, Tide+Gale = **Storm**).
- **Rank ladder** (Mushoku Tensei style): Novice → Adept → Warden → Saint → Sovereign → **Origin-touched**. Governs both card power tiers and player-progression cosmetics/titles (non-pay-to-win: rank is skill/mastery, not purchases).

### Oaths (the Nen restriction mechanic — our depth & drama layer)
- Before or during a duel, a summoner may swear an **Oath** — a self-imposed restriction — to amplify power. E.g., *"I shall summon no more than three monsters this duel"* → your monsters gain massive stats. *"I may not defend myself"* → your offense doubles. Break the Oath → a punishment triggers (life loss, silenced Aspect).
- This is optional, high-skill, and *intensely* dramatic — the exact "haha, you fool" theatrical beat, but mechanized. It's also endlessly analyzable ("is the Three-Monster Oath worth it vs a Gale deck?").

### Incantations (optional theater, skill-expressive)
- Summoning/casting works silently by default, but **reciting the incantation** (voice) grants a small bonus or faster cast — the anime shout, made mechanically meaningful but never *required* (public-play-friendly). Masters who "cast chantless" trade the bonus for stealth (opponent can't predict the summon).

## Part 5 — Why this is "talk-about-able"

- **Tier debates:** the wheel + hard limits guarantee matchup arguments (is Umbra oppressive? is Gale too fragile?).
- **Identity & cosplay:** six Aspects-as-cultures give players a "house" to belong to.
- **Theorycraft:** reactions + order-dependence + Oaths + Fusions = a deep meta to solve.
- **Lore engine:** the shattered Origin + Aspect feuds + the outlawing of Umbra = writers and players can build endless story.
- **Fits our XR game:** the "monsters are your conduit" universal cost mechanically enforces the summoner-supports-monsters role; reactions are spatial spectacle; incantations map to our voice-summon trigger; Oaths deliver the trap-card drama.
